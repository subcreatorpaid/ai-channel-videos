## 1. Capital Case

**Format:** 10 to 12 minute 2D cartoon explainers about money and power. Everything is drawn: bean-head characters, flat colour, muted sepia and navy palettes, black title cards for key terms ("DRAWBACK"), and short voiced character lines ("Where is Mr. Rockefeller?" / "He's unavailable."). One calm, serious male narrator. No stock footage or clips.

**Numbers:** 11.7k subs, 13 videos in about 9 months, no Shorts. Median views 54k, average 114k.

| Video | Length | Views | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rockefeller Explained in 10 Minutes | 10:03 | 379K | 101 |
| How the Great Depression Actually Happened (The Fatal Decisions) | 11:29 | 292K | 229 |
| Strait of Hormuz Explained Like You're 5 | 9:31 | 249K | 431 |
| The Mistake That Started World War 2 | 11:47 | 189K | 95 |
| The Rise (And Fall?) of BYD in 11 Minutes | 11:16 | 151K | 127 |
| Epstein: The $1.9 Billion Mystery Nobody Can Explain | 11:59 | 86K | 73 |
| The Rise of SpaceX / OpenAI / Rothschilds Explained in 11 Minutes | ~11 | 31K to 54K | 22 to 260 |
| BlackRock, FTX, Debt, Hyperinflation | ~12 | about 5K each | under 20 |

**Cadence:** one video every 2 to 4 weeks, with gaps of up to 7 weeks.

**Hook (first 30 seconds), the same every time:** a specific date and place, then a staggering fact, then an ironic reversal, then the thesis line "This is the story of how X... and [paradox]".
> "At its peak, one company controlled 90% of America's oil... The government spent 11 years trying to kill it, and did... Two years later, he was the richest human being in recorded history."

**Script and pacing:** about 150 words per minute, with 9 to 11 words per sentence. Each video has 4 or 5 named acts, listed as chapters in the description. A "but" turn comes every 60 to 90 seconds, and every act ends on a reversal. Each act has one voiced character exchange. There is one relatable vignette (the Ohio farmer) and one modern comparison (Rockefeller vs Bezos). The ending is a callback plus a one-word or one-line moral ("Order." / "They didn't get a monument. They got a policy.").

**Thumbnails:** one big ALL-CAPS name, 1 or 2 expressive caricatures, and one symbolic prop (a chain on the strait, a falling red chart).

**Comments:**
- Praise: "feels like a million-sub channel", it's clear without dumbing down, and it takes no political sides.
- Complaints: it gets compared to OverSimplified and Crayon Capital ("God I miss oversimplified", 239 likes; "almost carbon copy of Crayon Capital's Depression video"). People also point out typos ("Press Conferens"), a map error, and "(And Fall?)" read as clickbait.
- Some high-ranking comments look bot-written, so treat the comment counts as partly inflated.

**What works:** the art feels premium. It has the strongest hooks, a twist in every act, famous names explained through money mechanics, and timely news (Hormuz).

**What doesn't:** the cadence is uneven and there are no Shorts. The "Rise of X Explained in 11 Minutes" template is fading (31K to 54K). Abstract finance topics stall at about 5K. And viewers call the look derivative.

**Where the outliers come from:** a household name with a counterintuitive twist (the breakup made him richer), breaking news explained simply ("Like You're 5"), and titles that promise a "mistake" or "fatal decisions".

## 2. History of Simple Things

**Format:** 8 to 9 minute "why does everyday thing X work like this" videos. The visuals are AI-generated photoreal stills with slow pans and no characters, and the narrator sounds like AI. Every video uses the same intro ("Welcome back to History of Simple Things...") and the same outro.

**Numbers:** 1.04M subs, 240 uploads in 8 weeks (6 to 10 a day, posted on the hour). Median about 10K views. About 1 in 40 videos breaks out.

| Video | Length | Views | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Why Is Dry Cleaning Called Dry When Liquids Are Used | 8:05 | 195K | 45 |
| Why Do New Cars Keep Their Lights On During the Day? | 8:07 | 152K | 70 |
| Why Engineers Put Washers Under Bolts | 8:49 | 149K | 51 |
| How Much Electricity Does an Induction Cooktop Actually Use? | 8:30 | 133K | 129 |
| How Does Concrete Harden Underwater? | 8:11 | 131K | 7 |
| How Can a Retaining Wall Hold Back Millions of Pounds of Earth? | 8:34 | 130K | 24 |

**Hook:** "Have you ever wondered..." then the everyday thing, then the apparent contradiction, then "But...". It is generic and slow, and runs on title curiosity, not story. Some videos open by thanking the viewer who suggested the topic.

**Script and pacing:** 111 to 140 words per minute, with long sentences of 13 to 17 words. The same template every time: question, then "History of X", then how it works, then types and uses, then the conclusion. The one retention trick is holding the real answer back to about the 75% mark (in the car-lights video it comes at 6:04 of 8:07).

**Thumbnails:** one bright photoreal object, 2 to 4 words in white and yellow, and a red arrow. They're very clear at phone size.

**Comments:**
- Complaints: it feels padded ("8 minute video that says the washer distributes load, duh"), there are technical errors, and the captions cover on-screen labels.
- Many viewers suggest topics, which the shout-out drives.
- One video carried the chapter list from another video, a sign of mass production.

**What works:** huge topic volume, search-style "Why does X..." titles, clean thumbnails, comment-farming with the shout-out, and the delayed answer.

**What doesn't:** weak hooks, padding, AI look and voice, errors, and template slips. It's a lottery model.

**Where the outliers come from:** an everyday object with a built-in misconception or naming paradox (dry cleaning that uses liquid), the engineering behind ordinary things (washers, retaining walls), and money questions ("will it raise your bill?").

**Monetization risk:** high. Mass AI images, an AI voice and a templated script at 8 videos a day is the pattern that YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" update targets.

## 4. Replication blueprint for your niche (hidden workers and processes, CC graphic style)

**Topic rule:** a hidden job or overnight process people use every day, plus the scale or money behind it, plus one counterintuitive twist you can say in a single line. Take HoST's "everyday paradox" angle and tell it with CC's storytelling. For example:
- "The 4 AM Shift That Keeps Your Subway Alive" (twist: most of the subway is rebuilt every night).
- "Why Your Trash Disappears Before You Wake Up" (twist: garbage routes are planned like airline schedules).
- "The Mistake That Made Airports Run at Night".
- One news-peg video a month, for example a strike, a port jam or a blackout explained "like you're 5".

**Title:** [hidden place, job or process] plus [mistake / secret / why it works / nobody sees]. Keep it under 60 characters, and don't repeat one template every week.

**Thumbnail:** one ALL-CAPS word of 3 to 12 letters (white with a dark outline, at the top), 1 or 2 expressive caricature workers, one prop that shows the twist (a clock at 3 AM, a huge pile of trash, a red warning light), and a night-blue against warm-orange contrast. It must read in 1 second at phone size.

**Hook (0 to 30 seconds, about 75 words):** time and place ("3:12 AM, beneath Manhattan"), then a staggering fact, then an ironic reversal, then "This is the story of..." plus the paradox. No channel intro and no "welcome back".

**Script:**
- 1,500 to 1,700 words, which runs 10 to 11 minutes at about 150 words per minute. Keep sentences to 8 to 11 words.
- Use 4 or 5 named acts, and add them as chapters.
- End every act on a "but here's what nobody notices" turn. Aim for one every 60 to 90 seconds.
- Hold the biggest answer back to about 70 to 75% (HoST's trick).
- Give each act one voiced worker line, and add one human vignette (a single named night-shift worker) and one modern comparison.
- Close with a callback and a 3 to 6 word moral.

**Cadence:** one long video a week, steady (CC's gaps hurt). Add 2 or 3 Shorts a week, each cut from the best twist in that week's long video.

**Community:** end with "Which hidden job should we follow next? We'll credit you on screen" (HoST's tactic), and pin that question.

**Avoid:**
- From HoST: mass AI imagery, an AI-sounding voice, templated intros, 8 uploads a day, padded runtime, unexplained visuals, and captions over labels.
- From CC: typos and map errors (proofread every on-screen word), long gaps, and a look that copies OverSimplified or Crayon Capital.

**Staying monetized (reused and inauthentic content rules):**
1. **Your own art, not CC's.** Use CC's approach (flat 2D, caricatures, title cards) with your own character design: a different head shape, line weight, palette and fonts. Never reuse CC, OverSimplified or Crayon Capital assets, music or thumbnails. Viewers already call CC a copy.
2. **Original scripts.** Write every script from primary sources (city reports, union and worker interviews, company docs, news) and never paraphrase another channel's video. Where a topic has been covered before, find a new angle.
3. **No templated videos.** Draw scenes for each video and vary the layouts. Keep the upload pace one a human team could produce, which 1 long video plus 2 or 3 Shorts a week is.
4. **Voice.** Use a consistent, custom-sounding voice that's edited for pacing. Never paste raw stock TTS. (With Kokoro or another AI voice, edit the script and pacing by hand.)
5. **Real footage only in short, transformed clips.** Frame it, annotate it, keep it under your own narration, and credit it in the description.
6. **AI disclosure.** If any photorealistic AI scene appears, switch on the "altered or synthetic" toggle. Flat cartoon art doesn't need it.
7. **Keep a proof folder for every video** (sources, script drafts, scene files), in case you ever have to appeal.
8. **Sources and a disclaimer in every description.** Avoid accusatory framing about real people or companies.
